## 2. The audience, from sourced data

Every claim in this section carries its source; these claims are reused by the concept cards.

- **A-1. Sports betting is a mass US behavior, male- and young-skewed.** 19% of US adults bet on sports in the prior year (Pew, 2022: 6,034 adults); 24% of men vs 15% of women; concentrated under 50. Source: Pew Research Center, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2022/09/14/as-more-states-legalize-the-practice-19-of-u-s-adults-say-they-have-bet-money-on-sports-in-the-past-year/ [verified live]; AP coverage with the male/female split: https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/1-in-5-u-s-adults-bet-money-on-sports-in-past-year-according-to-study/ [verified live].
- **A-2. The under-30 male core keeps growing.** 31% of adults under 30 placed a sports bet in the prior year (36% of men under 30), per Pew's 2025 follow-up: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/02/americans-increasingly-see-legal-sports-betting-as-a-bad-thing-for-society-and-sports/ [verified live]. Half of US men 18–49 (52%) hold an active online sportsbook account (Siena Research Institute / St. Bonaventure, April 2026): https://sri.siena.edu/2026/04/13/more-than-a-quarter-of-americans-27-have-an-active-online-sports-betting-account-a-third-have-opened-an-account-at-least-once/ [verified live].
- **A-3. The audience frames wagers as skill and research, not luck.** 85% of online bettors say they enjoy researching statistics on players and teams to bet more successfully; 83% enjoy small-stake, high-multiple bets (parlays/futures) (Siena/SRI 2026, same URL as A-2) [verified live]. This is the audience the *outsmart for real money* message was validated on [TESTED, R30] — the claim and the calibration agree.
- **A-4. Prediction markets are converging with sports betting for the same users.** Prediction-market activity reached 27% of legal US sports-betting volume during the 2026 World Cup, up from 9% at the start of the year, and one in five Americans frame sports betting as an investment (two in five for Gen Z) — Bank of America Institute data via Fortune: https://fortune.com/2026/09/07/americans-sports-gambling-investment-gen-z-75-cents-dollar-wagered/ [verified live].
- **A-5. Betting is habitual and daily for a large minority.** Nearly a quarter of sports bettors wager daily and another third weekly (BofA Institute via Fortune, A-4 URL) [verified live]. 60% of online bettors have chased a bet; 63% have bet ≥$100 in one day (Siena/SRI 2026, A-2 URL) [verified live].
- **A-6. Combat sports and football dominate the betting slate.** Football leads US betting interest by a wide margin, with boxing among the popular secondary markets (Statista Consumer Insights 2024): https://www.statista.com/chart/33925/share-of-us-respondents-bet-on-sports/ [verified live].

## 3. Presentation-code survey (the six territories R14 names)

### 3.1 Sportsbook & DFS presentation codes
Surveyed properties: DraftKings (https://www.draftkings.com/ [verified live]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DraftKings [verified live]), FanDuel (https://www.fanduel.com/ [verified live]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FanDuel [verified live]), BetMGM (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BetMGM [verified live]), PrizePicks (https://www.prizepicks.com/ [verified live]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrizePicks [verified live]), Underdog Fantasy (https://underdogfantasy.com/ [verified live]).

Recurring codes: **near-black grounds with one saturated brand accent** (DraftKings green-on-black, PrizePicks purple-on-dark, Underdog gold-on-dark); **data-dense mono/condensed numerals**; odds framed as **player choices, not house offers**; celebratory win states in green, losses stated flatly; **countdown clocks everywhere** (lock times as appointment mechanics); parlay builders as *construction* interfaces (the A-3 research-pleasure claim made into UI). The artifact's fixed frame (R11, R79: display/data/prose roles, near-black ground, single accent) is already inside this code family — a treatment succeeds by choosing an accent and vocabulary this audience already reads as "money interface."

### 3.2 Combat-sport broadcast design
Surveyed properties: UFC (https://www.ufc.com/ [verified live]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Fighting_Championship [verified live]), crossover boxing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Paul [verified live]), Power Slap as the minimal-format extreme (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Slap [verified live]).

Recurring codes: **the fight card** (main event / undercard hierarchy — the artifact's bracket-and-tier ladder is structurally a fight card, R56/R62); **tale of the tape** (two columns of factual stats face to face — the artifact's opponent tendency card, R81, is one); gold-on-black championship iconography; the **record as identity** ("22-1" says everything — the artifact's per-tier W/L records, R60, are this code verbatim); ringside-scorekeeper neutrality in graphics packages. This is the single strongest code overlap with the fixed mechanics: brackets, sealed simultaneous commitment, permanent records, and a floor that ends careers map one-to-one onto fight-sport furniture.

### 3.3 Fantasy sports
Surveyed properties: fantasy football as the base habit (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_football_(gridiron) [verified live]), DFS platforms above (§3.1).

Recurring codes: **roster attachment** (my guys), weekly **appointment cadence**, head-to-head record-keeping, research-before-commitment as the core loop (A-3). Maps to the manifest cards R26(1) scouting reports and R26(4) marquee scheduled brackets.

### 3.4 Sports-card collecting
Surveyed properties: Topps (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topps [verified live]), Panini (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panini_Group [verified live]), trading cards generally (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_card [verified live]).

Recurring codes: **the card as the atomic unit of a competitor** (portrait, name, stats, rarity frame); the ledgered permanence of a record (a card is a permanent stat line); grading/serial culture = provenance obsession. The artifact's character card with name, element, per-tier record (R60, R81, R82) is a sports card; a treatment should style it as one.

### 3.5 Betting-streamer culture
Surveyed properties: Stake.com and its streamer economy (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stake.com [verified live]), Kick, the platform built around it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick_(service) [verified live]), Barstool Sports as betting-adjacent media (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barstool_Sports [verified live]), Action Network as picks-and-odds media (https://www.actionnetwork.com/ [verified live]).

Recurring codes: **sweat as content** (watching someone else's stake at risk is entertainment — supports R26(3) spectate); big-multiplier celebration moments; personality-first presentation (the streamer is a named character with a record); **degen self-irony** in copy voice — this audience jokes about its own losses. In-frame copy that is dry about losing (the incumbent Narrator's register, R71) is native to this culture.
